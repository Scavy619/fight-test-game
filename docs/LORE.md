# Mythic Neon: lore

Every fighter and arena below has two labelled parts:

- **Tradition**: what the source mythology, religion or martial art actually says, summarised from the sources listed. Where sources disagree, this is noted.
- **In this game**: our own fiction. Nothing in these parts is traditional unless it says so.

Respect rules (SPEC section 7): deities worshipped today are patrons only. They are never playable, never shown being struck or defeated, and never the target of jokes. Legendary heroes and tricksters long depicted as fighters in popular retellings may appear as themselves.

The in-game lore screen reads a tightened copy of the fighter and arena text from `src/content/lore.ts`. Keep the two in step.

## World premise (in this game)

Far in the future, humanity's surviving archives of myth, epic and martial knowledge are kept by an AI called the Concord. To stop those stories from fading, the Concord stages a tournament inside a city of light, rebuilding legends from everything people ever wrote, carved or sang about them. Heroes and tricksters return as themselves, rendered in neon because they are made of stored light and data. The great gods are not rebuilt: the Concord holds that what is still worshipped is not its to copy, so the gods take part only by lending power to mortal champions who carry their blessing into the arena.

## Fighters

### Rei Kurogane

Kenjutsu and ninjutsu swordsman. Patron: Susanoo. Original character.

**Tradition**

- Susanoo (Susanoo-no-Mikoto) is a Shinto kami, younger brother of the sun goddess Amaterasu. The Kojiki (c. 712) and Nihon Shoki (720) tell of his birth from the god Izanagi and link him to storms and the sea. He is wild and impetuous: after a dispute with Amaterasu and a rampage in her realm, the gods expel him from the High Plain of Heaven (Takamagahara).
- In Izumo he meets an elderly couple whose daughters have been taken by the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi. He protects the last daughter, Kushinadahime, gets the serpent drunk on strong sake and kills it. In its tail he finds a sword, later called Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi, which he gives to Amaterasu. The hero Yamato Takeru later uses it to escape a grass fire, the source of its name "grass-cutter". It is one of Japan's three Imperial Regalia, believed to be kept at Atsuta Shrine in Nagoya and never shown in public.
- Susanoo is worshipped today, for example at Yasaka Shrine in Kyoto (home of the Gion Matsuri), Suga Shrine and Susa Shrine in Shimane, and the Hikawa shrines around Tokyo.
- Kenjutsu is the umbrella term for the older Japanese sword schools, many founded in the Muromachi period (1336 to 1573), such as Tenshin Shoden Katori Shinto-ryu. Training centres on paired forms (kata) with wooden swords. Kendo is its later sport form, built on bamboo swords and armour from the 19th century. The katana, a curved single-edged blade over about 60 cm worn edge-up through the belt, emerged in the 14th century.
- Ninjutsu names the skills of the shinobi: spies, scouts and raiders, notably from the self-governing Iga and Koka areas during the Sengoku period. The black costume and many popular feats are later inventions with no historical evidence, and modern schools claiming a ninja lineage are disputed. Shuriken ("hidden hand blades") were small spikes or flat plates thrown mainly to distract, and were taught in samurai schools as well as ninja ones.

**In this game**

- Rei Kurogane is a young swordsman trained in a kenjutsu school and a shinobi tradition that the Concord rebuilt from old manuals. Susanoo chose him as his champion, so wind and storm follow his blade.
- His shuriken and his footwork come from the real arts. The wind-slash dash and the shadow step teleport are our inventions, drawn from Susanoo's storms and from the legendary image of the ninja.
- His katana is his own sword, not Kusanagi. His ultimate, Eightfold Storm, is a rush of eight wind-driven slashes that recalls the eight-headed serpent of the Orochi legend; Susanoo himself never appears in battle.

### Vira

Kalaripayattu spear fighter. Patron: Kartikeya (Murugan, Skanda). Original character.

**Tradition**

- Kartikeya, also called Skanda, Murugan, Subrahmanya and Kumara, is the Hindu god of war and commander of the gods' army, son of Shiva and Parvati. In the Skanda Purana, six sparks from Shiva become six infants raised by the Krittikas (the Pleiades); Parvati unites them into one child with six faces (Shanmukha). His name Kartikeya means "of the Krittikas".
- He is shown as a youth riding or standing beside a peacock, with a rooster on his banner, holding the vel, a divine spear given to him by his mother Parvati as an embodiment of her power. With it he defeats the asuras Taraka and Surapadman. The vel is itself worshipped in his temples, and the spear was a prominent weapon in ancient Tamil Sangam literature.
- Murugan is central to Tamil devotion today, with the Six Abodes (Arupadai Veedu) in Tamil Nadu and major festivals such as Thaipusam, Karthigai Deepam and Soorasamharam, observed in India, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Singapore and the wider Tamil diaspora.
- Kalaripayattu is the martial art of Kerala, practised since at least the 12th century, with older roots in Tamil Sangam-era combat and the Sanskrit Dhanur Veda. Training takes place in a kalari and moves from body conditioning and animal-named stances to wooden weapons, metal weapons (spear, sword and shield, dagger, mace, the flexible urumi sword) and finally empty hands. It includes knowledge of vital points (marma) and healing massage. The British banned it in 1804; teachers kept it alive in secret and revived it publicly from the 1920s.

**In this game**

- Vira is a Kalaripayattu fighter from a kalari rebuilt in the Concord's city, trained through the full sequence of stances, staff and spear. Kartikeya chose her as his champion, and his fire runs along her spear.
- Her spear lunge and long reach come from the real art. Her spear is shaped after the vel but is her own weapon, not the divine vel. The Spiral Orb and the leaping flame arc are our inventions.
- Her ultimate, Vel of the Six Fires, rains spears of light in honour of the six sparks of Kartikeya's birth. Kartikeya is never shown fighting or being struck.

### Sun Wukong

The Monkey King, with his staff. Appears as himself.

**Tradition**

- Sun Wukong is the Monkey King of Journey to the West, a 16th-century Chinese novel usually attributed to Wu Cheng'en. Born from a magic stone on the Mountain of Flowers and Fruit, he learns the 72 transformations from a Taoist master. One cloud somersault carries him 108,000 li, and he can turn plucked hairs into copies of himself.
- His weapon, the Ruyi Jingu Bang, is an iron pillar with gold bands at each end, said to weigh 13,500 jin. It once measured the depth of the great flood and lay in the treasury of the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea. It grows or shrinks at his command; he keeps it as a needle behind his ear.
- Given a lowly post in Heaven, he rebels, calls himself Great Sage Equal to Heaven and defeats the heavenly armies (the episode known as Havoc in Heaven). The Buddha traps him under a mountain for 500 years. Freed, he protects the monk Tang Sanzang on his pilgrimage to India for Buddhist scriptures and ends the journey as the Victorious Fighting Buddha. He is also venerated at some temples and shrines in Chinese communities.
- The staff (gun) is one of the four major weapons of Chinese martial arts, called the "grandfather of all weapons". Staff routines are a main event (gunshu) in modern competitive wushu.

**In this game**

- The Concord rebuilt Sun Wukong from the novel and the stories told about him since, and he returns as himself: quick, proud and fond of a fight.
- His moves follow the novel: the clone strike uses his hair clones, the cloud dash his cloud somersault, and the pillar slam and Havoc in Heaven ultimate use the staff's power to change size. His staff technique draws on modern wushu staff forms.

### Thor

Thunder god with his hammer and glima wrestling. Appears as himself.

**Tradition**

- Thor is the Norse god of thunder and the sky, defender of the gods' realm Asgard and the human realm Midgard, mostly against giants. Our main sources are the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda (c. 1220, attributed to Snorri Sturluson). He rides a chariot drawn by two goats, wears the strength-doubling belt Megingjord and the iron gloves he needs to grip his hammer. His great enemy is the Midgard Serpent: at Ragnarok he kills it and dies of its venom.
- His hammer Mjolnir was forged by the dwarf brothers Brokkr and Eitri (or Sindri). It never misses, returns to his hand, can be made small enough to carry inside his shirt, and has a short handle. It was also used to bless brides, funerals and Thor's goats. About a thousand hammer pendants have been found across the Viking world.
- In one Prose Edda tale Thor wrestles an old woman, Elli, at the hall of the giant Utgarda-Loki and is forced to one knee; she is old age itself in disguise. Thor and the other Norse gods are honoured today in modern Heathen religions, such as Iceland's Asatruarfelagid, recognised by the state in 1973.
- Glima is Icelandic folk wrestling. Medieval Icelandic law books and sagas mention wrestling; the modern sport uses belts around the waist and thighs, an upright stance and a clockwise stepping rhythm, and is governed by a code of honour (drengskapur). It is Iceland's national sport.

**In this game**

- The Concord rebuilt Thor from the Eddas and he appears as himself: patient, loud and protective of the weak.
- His hammer throw returns to his hand, as Mjolnir does in the myths, and his ultimate, Mjolnir's Judgment, calls down a column of lightning. Making Thor a glima wrestler is our invention, drawn from the Icelandic sport and from the tale of his wrestling match with Elli; the charging shoulder is invented.

## Arenas

### Storm Shrine (Japan)

**Tradition**

- A torii is the gate at the entrance to a Shinto shrine, marking the boundary between the everyday world and sacred ground; many are painted vermilion. The torii of Itsukushima Shrine stands in the sea.
- A shimenawa is a rope of twisted rice straw or hemp, often hung with zigzag paper streamers (shide), that marks a sacred or purified space on shrines, torii, trees and rocks. Myth links it to Amaterasu's return from the cave where she hid after her quarrel with Susanoo. Japan's largest hangs at Izumo Taisha.
- Susanoo is linked to storms and the sea, and to the Izumo region, where he defeated Yamata no Orochi.

**In this game**

- The Storm Shrine is the Concord's reconstruction of a sea shrine dedicated to Susanoo: neon torii stand in dark water under rain, with shimenawa strung between them and lightning on the horizon. Fighters duel on the stone approach, never inside the sanctuary.

### Lamp Citadel (India)

**Tradition**

- A gopuram is the monumental entrance tower of a South Indian (Dravidian) Hindu temple: a tapering, many-storeyed tower over the gateway in the temple wall, covered with painted sculpture of the temple's myths and topped by finials (kalasam). Gopurams grew to dominate temple skylines between the 12th and 16th centuries.
- Karthigai Deepam is a Tamil festival of lamps on the full moon of the month Karthigai (November or December), when homes and temples are lined with clay oil lamps. It honours the birth of Murugan, whose six infant forms were raised by the Krittikas, and Shiva's appearance as a column of fire; a great lamp is lit on Arunachala hill at Tiruvannamalai. The festival is mentioned in Sangam-era Tamil poetry.

**In this game**

- The Lamp Citadel is a temple city at dusk, rebuilt by the Concord from South Indian architecture, its gopurams outlined in neon. During its eternal Karthigai night, rows of oil lamps line the walls and embers drift up toward the towers. Fighters meet in the outer courtyard, never in the shrines.

### Bifrost Bridge (Norse)

**Tradition**

- Bifrost (Bilrost in the Poetic Edda) is the bridge between Midgard and Asgard. The sources call it burning and three-coloured; it is usually read as a rainbow, and some scholars suggest the Milky Way. The god Heimdall guards it from his hall Himinbjorg. The gods ride over it every day to their council at the well of Urd, while Thor wades through rivers instead. At Ragnarok it breaks under the riders from Muspell.
- Yggdrasil is the immense ash tree at the centre of Norse cosmology, with the worlds around it, three roots over three wells, the Norns watering it, and an eagle, a squirrel, a serpent and four stags living in and under it. It shakes as Ragnarok approaches.

**In this game**

- The Concord renders Bifrost as a bridge of crystal light under aurora bands, with snow falling and the shadow of Yggdrasil across the sky. The bridge's red band still burns, as the Eddas say. It is the one arena where the Concord lets the archive's ending show: faint cracks run along the bridge.
