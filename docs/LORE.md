# ASTRA: lore

ASTRA is a **fictional fantasy adaptation inspired by epic traditions**. It is not a retelling of any tradition, text or regional version, and it does not claim to be canonical. Each hero below has two labelled parts:

- **Tradition**: what the sources say, summarised from the references listed at the end. Where tellings differ, this is noted.
- **In this game**: our own fiction. Nothing in these parts is traditional unless it says so.

Respect rules (SPEC section 7): the heroes are epic warriors, playable as themselves. Deities worshipped today (Shiva, Vishnu, Durga, Hanuman, Rama and others) are never playable, never struck, never villains and never the target of a joke. No hero is mocked, and the antagonist is a person with motives, not a monster.

## World premise (in this game)

Across several realms, something is going wrong with the legendary weapons. Bows misfire, maces ring with a power their owners did not give them, and kingdoms that once kept peace begin to quarrel. The heroes learn that a hidden force, the Hollow Astra, is amplifying the weapons so that warriors strike without judgment. The campaign is about courage, duty, loyalty, restraint, and what power costs when no one stops to ask whether it should be used.

## Heroes

Arjuna and Bhima are written. Karna, Abhimanyu, Ghatotkacha, Parashurama, Ekalavya and Ashwatthama are written with their hero slices (see `docs/PLAN.md`).

### Arjuna, the Astra-Bound Archer

**Tradition**

- Arjuna is the third of the five Pandava brothers in the Mahabharata, a master archer trained by the teacher Drona. His bow is the Gandiva, which the sources say he receives from the fire god Agni during the burning of the Khandava forest, together with quivers that never empty.
- He wins Draupadi at her svayamvara by an archery feat, and later gains divine weapons called astras after a long penance. The Bhagavad Gita, set on the eve of the Kurukshetra war, begins with Arjuna's doubt and his refusal to fight kin; the teaching that follows is among the most studied passages in Hindu thought.
- Retellings differ on details of the Khandava episode and of his travels; this adaptation keeps only the broad outline.

**In this game**

- Arjuna is a gifted archer who has trained until the Gandiva feels like part of his own judgment. When a corrupted blast threatens an ancient forest he refuses to fire blindly, traces the disturbance, and lowers his bow to protect the trees before pursuing the source.
- The astras of this game are our own idea: charged arrows and barrages with long, visible warnings. Gods do not appear in his story. His doubts are shown in short moments before each duel, and he answers them with focus and humility.
- His campaign chapter tests whether he can act with resolve without losing compassion. His rival is Karna.

### Bhima, the Mountain's Strength

**Tradition**

- Bhima is the second Pandava, described in the Mahabharata as the son of the wind god Vayu, known for enormous strength and his gada (mace). He kills the rakshasa Hidimba and marries Hidimba's sister Hidimbi; their son is Ghatotkacha. In the war he fights Duryodhana in the mace duel that ends it.
- He is also remembered for his appetite, his loyalty to his brothers, and for working as a cook at King Virata's court during the Pandavas' year in disguise.
- Details of these episodes differ between tellings and regions.

**In this game**

- Bhima's strength is direct but never cruel. At a collapsing mountain gate he braces the beams long enough for villagers to escape, then turns to face what drove them there.
- He fights close and heavy with the gada and wrestler's holds. His campaign asks whether he can protect the people he loves without letting anger dictate each blow. Warmth, loyalty and humour are as much his character as his strength.
